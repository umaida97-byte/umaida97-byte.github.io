Selling Property

Should I Sell or Rent My Property?

A common question with no universal answer - it comes down to your finances, plans and appetite for being a landlord.

By Five Towns Lettings. Last reviewed October 2026. General information for landlords in England, not legal or financial advice.

Whether to sell a property or let it out is one of the most common questions we're asked, often after an inheritance, a move or a relationship change. There's no single right answer, but there are clear questions that will point you in the right direction.

Reasons to sell

  • You need the money for another purchase or to clear debts.
  • You don't want the responsibility of being a landlord.
  • The property would need significant work before it could be let safely.
  • The rent wouldn't cover the mortgage and running costs with a comfortable margin.
  • You may want to sell within the next year or two, which is harder with a tenant in place (see below).

Reasons to rent it out

  • You can afford to hold the property and the rent covers your costs.
  • Local rental demand is strong for that type of property.
  • You want a long-term income and to benefit from any future growth in value.
  • You're comfortable managing a tenancy yourself, or paying an agent to do it.

Questions to ask before you decide

What will the numbers look like?

Get a sale valuation from a local estate agent and a free rental valuation from us. Then list the yearly costs of letting: mortgage interest, insurance, safety certificates, maintenance, management fees and an allowance for empty periods. Our rental yield guide shows how to turn those figures into a net return.

What about the mortgage?

If the property has a residential mortgage, you'll need your lender's consent to let, or a buy-to-let mortgage, which may have a different rate.

What about tax?

Rental profit is taxable income, and selling a property that isn't your main home can bring a capital gains tax bill. How mortgage interest is treated also depends on whether you own the property personally or through a company. Speak to an accountant before you decide, because the answer depends on your own circumstances.

How long do you plan to keep it?

This matters more since the Renters' Rights Act took effect on 1 May 2026. Tenancies are now periodic with no fixed end date, and section 21 has ended. If you later want to sell, you'll need to use the selling ground, which generally can't be used in the first year of a tenancy and needs four months' notice. If you think you'll want to sell soon, it may make sense to sell now.

Are you ready to be a landlord?

Letting comes with legal responsibilities, from deposit protection to annual gas safety checks. Read our guide to self-management and our compliance checklist to see what's involved.

A middle option

Some owners let the property for a while to give themselves time to decide, or to wait for a better time to sell. That can work, as long as you are comfortable with the notice periods above and have a tenant who will look after the property.

Talk it through

We specialise in lettings and don't sell property, so we have no reason to push you either way. Tell us about the property and we'll give you an honest rental figure and a view on how it would let. If you decide to let, we can find the tenant or manage the whole tenancy.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to sell or rent out my house?

It depends on whether you need the money now, whether the rent covers your costs with a margin, your tax position, and how long you plan to keep the property. Get both a sale valuation and a rental valuation and compare the net figures.

Can I sell my rental property with a tenant in it?

Yes, but since 1 May 2026 landlords in England who want vacant possession to sell must generally use the selling ground, which can't usually be used in the first year of a tenancy and requires four months' notice. You can also sell with the tenant in place to another landlord.
