The Hanley rental market

Hanley has the most varied housing in the city:

  • City-centre apartments. Over the past decade, new builds and conversions of older commercial buildings have added a lot of one- and two-bedroom flats near the Cultural Quarter and the shopping centre.
  • Victorian terraces on the edges of the centre, in Shelton, Northwood, Joiner's Square and Birches Head.
  • Family homes further out, towards Bucknall, Bentilee and Sneyd Green.

Who rents in Hanley: young professionals and people who work in the city centre, students and staff at Staffordshire University (whose main campus is a short walk away in Shelton), and people who don't drive, because Hanley is the hub of the city's bus network.

Advice for Hanley landlords

  • Check service charges and the lease before buying an apartment. Ground rent and service charges come straight out of your yield, and some leases restrict subletting or short tenancies. We are happy to look over the numbers before you buy.
  • Plan for higher turnover. City-centre tenants move more often. Good photos, quick viewings and sensible pricing keep empty periods short. See our guide to reducing void periods.
  • Students and HMOs. Shared houses near the university need the right licence and safety standards. Our HMO investment guide explains what is involved.

Living in Hanley

Hanley has the city's main shopping (The Potteries Centre), Hanley bus station, and the Cultural Quarter: the Victoria Hall, the Regent Theatre, and the Potteries Museum & Art Gallery, home to part of the Staffordshire Hoard. Hanley Park and Central Forest Park give tenants green space close to the centre, and the Festival Park leisure area in Etruria is a short drive away. Stoke-on-Trent railway station, on the West Coast Main Line, is about five minutes away by car or bus.
