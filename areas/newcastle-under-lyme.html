The Newcastle-under-Lyme rental market

  • Town centre: Georgian and Victorian buildings, flats above shops and conversions, popular with young professionals and Keele students
  • Westlands, Clayton and Thistleberry: sought-after family areas with larger interwar and post-war homes, where rents are higher and tenants stay longer
  • Wolstanton, May Bank and Porthill: Victorian and Edwardian terraces and semis, popular with couples and young families
  • Silverdale, Knutton, Chesterton and Cross Heath: more affordable terraces and former council homes with steady local demand
  • Keele: demand from university staff and postgraduate students
  • Kidsgrove: terraces and family homes in the north of the borough, with its own railway station

Advice for Newcastle landlords

  • Different council, different rules. Newcastle-under-Lyme is run by its own borough council, not Stoke-on-Trent City Council. Licensing and planning rules for shared houses can differ, so check before buying or converting. We will confirm the current rules for your property.
  • School catchments drive family demand. Family homes in popular school catchments let quickly and keep tenants for longer.
  • Student lets need planning. Keele University creates strong demand, but student lets follow the academic year, so timing your marketing matters. See our HMO guide.

Living in Newcastle-under-Lyme

Tenants like Newcastle's walkable town centre, with its Guildhall, market stalls, Queen's Gardens, shops and restaurants. Road links are excellent: the A34, A53 and A525 meet here, and the M6 is reachable at junctions 15 and 16. Keele University is just west of the town, and buses run frequently to Hanley and Stoke-on-Trent station.
