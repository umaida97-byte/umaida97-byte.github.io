The Fenton rental market

Fenton's housing is mostly Victorian and Edwardian terraces around Albert Square, Fenton Low and Lane Delph, with more post-war family homes in Mount Pleasant, Heron Cross and Berry Hill.

There are fewer rental properties here than in the bigger towns, so a well-kept family home in the right street rarely sits empty for long. Demand comes from local families who want an affordable home with easy access to Stoke and Longton, and from people working at the business parks along the A50 and A500 and around Trentham Lakes.

Advice for Fenton landlords

  • Less stock, less competition. Fewer investors look at Fenton, so buying is often less competitive than in Longton or Hanley, while the tenants you attract are similar.
  • Look at Fenton and Longton together. If you are buying, the two towns share a similar tenant pool and price range. Our property sourcing service can compare specific properties in both.
  • Family-friendly finishes. A secure garden, a decent kitchen and good storage count for more here than city-centre extras.

Living in Fenton

Fenton sits between Stoke and Longton, with the A50 and A500 both close by, so it is an easy drive to the M6, Derby and the city centre. Fenton Manor sports complex has swimming and fitness facilities, the bet365 Stadium and Trentham Lakes are just south, and Fenton Town Hall on Albert Square is the centre of the old town. Regular buses run to Hanley, Stoke and Longton.

Fenton FAQs

Is Fenton one of the six towns of Stoke-on-Trent?

Yes. Arnold Bennett wrote about the Five Towns and left Fenton out, but it is one of the six towns that joined together to form the county borough of Stoke-on-Trent in 1910.
