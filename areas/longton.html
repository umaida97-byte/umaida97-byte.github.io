The Longton rental market

Longton grew up around the bone china industry, and the housing still reflects that. The streets closest to the town centre are mostly two- and three-bedroom Victorian terraces built for pottery workers. Further out, Meir, Meir Hay, Lightwood and Weston Coyney have more interwar and post-war semi-detached homes with gardens and driveways.

Tenant demand is steady and mostly local:

  • Working families who want the bigger semis in Meir and Lightwood and the schools around them
  • Couples and first-time renters who want an affordable terrace within walking distance of the town centre and the station
  • Commuters heading towards Derby, Uttoxeter and the A50 corridor, which runs right past the town

Advice for Longton landlords

  • Older terraces need staying on top of. Many Longton terraces are over 100 years old. Damp, roofs, older wiring and single-glazed rear windows are the most common issues we see. Keeping on top of them protects the rent and keeps you compliant with the EICR, gas safety and EPC rules. Our landlord compliance guide sets out what is needed.
  • Each street is different. Two terraces a few minutes apart can let very differently. We'll give you an honest view of rent and demand for your specific street, not just the area average.
  • First-time investors. Low entry prices make Longton a common first buy-to-let. If you are still at the buying stage, see our buy-to-let guide.

Living in Longton

Tenants like Longton for its practical town centre. The Longton Exchange shopping centre and the high street cover day-to-day needs. Longton railway station is on the Crewe to Derby line, and the A50 gives fast road access east to Derby and west to the M6. Queen's Park, one of the city's Victorian parks, is on the edge of the town, and the Gladstone Pottery Museum, one of the last complete Victorian potbank sites, is a reminder of the area's heritage.
